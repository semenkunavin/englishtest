Comparative Adjectives: bigger, more interesting (A2)

We use comparative adjectives to compare two people, things or places. Short adjectives add -er (big → bigger), and longer adjectives use more (interesting → more interesting). The word than often follows the comparison.

  1. One-syllable adjectives: add -er + than: tall → taller than, old → older than.
  2. Adjectives ending in consonant + -y: change y to i and add -er: happy → happier, easy → easier.
  3. Short adjectives ending in consonant + vowel + consonant double the last letter: big → bigger, hot → hotter.
  4. Longer adjectives (two or more syllables) use more + adjective: more interesting, more expensive, more beautiful.
  5. Irregular forms: good → better, bad → worse, far → further.
  6. Do not use more and -er together: say more interesting OR cheaper, never more cheaper.

Examples

  • My brother is taller than me.My brother is taller than me.
  • This book is more interesting than the film.This book is more interesting than the film.
  • Today is hotter than yesterday.Today is hotter than yesterday.
  • Her new phone is more expensive than mine.Her new phone is more expensive than mine.
  • I think maths is easier than English.I think maths is easier than English.
  • The weather this week is better than last week.The weather this week is better than last week.

Typical mistakes

This bag is more cheaper than that one.
This bag is cheaper than that one.
Use -er or more, not both together.
She is more tall than her sister.
She is taller than her sister.
Short adjectives like tall take -er, not more.
My test score is gooder than yours.
My test score is better than yours.
Good is irregular: the comparative is better.
