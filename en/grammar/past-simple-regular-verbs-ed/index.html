Past Simple: Regular Verbs (-ed) in English

The past simple talks about actions that finished in the past, for example yesterday or last week. With regular verbs, you make the past form by adding -ed to the verb. The same form is used for all persons: I, you, he, she, it, we, they.

  1. Add -ed to the base verb: work → worked, play → played, watch → watched.
  2. If the verb ends in -e, add only -d: live → lived, like → liked.
  3. If the verb ends in a consonant + y, change y to i and add -ed: study → studied, carry → carried.
  4. After a short stressed vowel + consonant, double the consonant: stop → stopped, plan → planned.
  5. The form is the same for every person: I worked, she worked, they worked.
  6. Negatives and questions use did + base verb, not -ed: I didn't work, Did you work?

Examples

  • I watched a film last night.I watched a film last night.
  • She studied English for two hours.She studied English for two hours.
  • We stopped at a small café.We stopped at a small café.
  • They didn't play football yesterday.They didn't play football yesterday.
  • Did you finish your homework?Did you finish your homework?
  • He lived in London when he was a child.He lived in London when he was a child.

Typical mistakes

I didn't watched TV.
I didn't watch TV.
After didn't, use the base verb, not the -ed form.
Did she finished the work?
Did she finish the work?
After did, use the base verb, not the -ed form.
He studyed all evening.
He studied all evening.
With a consonant + y, change y to i before -ed.
