Subject Pronouns in English: I, you, he, she, it, we, they

Subject pronouns are short words that replace the name of a person or thing in front of a verb: I, you, he, she, it, we, they. We use them to avoid repeating nouns: we say She is my sister, not Anna is my sister again and again. The subject pronoun always comes before the verb.

  1. Use I for yourself. It is always a capital letter, even in the middle of a sentence: My brother and I are students.
  2. Use you for one person or for many people: You are my friend. / You are my friends.
  3. Use he for a man or boy, she for a woman or girl, and it for a thing, an animal or an idea: He is a teacher. She is a doctor. It is a cat.
  4. Use we for yourself plus other people: We are a family.
  5. Use they for two or more people or things: They are at school.
  6. The pronoun is the subject of the sentence, so it comes before the verb: They live in London. Do not add a noun after it: She is happy, not She Anna is happy.

Examples

  • I am from Canada.I am from Canada.
  • You are a good student.You are a good student.
  • He works in a bank.He works in a bank.
  • She likes coffee.She likes coffee.
  • It is very cold today.It is very cold today.
  • We live in a small house, and they live next to us.We live in a small house, and they live next to us.

Typical mistakes

My mother is a nurse. He works in a hospital.
My mother is a nurse. She works in a hospital.
Use she for a woman or girl, not he.
My brother he is ten years old.
My brother is ten years old. / He is ten years old.
Use the noun or the pronoun, not both together.

  2. Maria is my sister. ___ is twelve.

  3. My dog is small. ___ is brown.

  4. Tom and Anna are my friends. ___ are nice.

  5. My father and I are at home. ___ are in the kitchen.
