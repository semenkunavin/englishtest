Past Simple of "To Be": Was and Were (A1)

The past simple of to be is was or were. We use it to talk about people, things and situations in the past, for example yesterday, last week or in 2010. We also use it to say how old someone was or where someone was.

  1. Use was with I, he, she and it: I was tired.
  2. Use were with you, we and they: They were at home.
  3. Negative: add not — was not (wasn't) and were not (weren't).
  4. Questions: put was or were before the subject: Were you happy?
  5. Short answers: Yes, I was. / No, I wasn't. / Yes, they were. / No, they weren't.
  6. Do not use did with to be: say Was she at school?, not Did she was at school?.

Examples

  • I was at home yesterday.I was at home yesterday.
  • She was tired after work.She was tired after work.
  • We were in London last year.We were in London last year.
  • They weren't at school on Monday.They weren't at school on Monday.
  • Was he your teacher?Was he your teacher?
  • My parents were very happy.My parents were very happy.

Typical mistakes

I were at home yesterday.
I was at home yesterday.
With I we use was, not were.
They was at the park.
They were at the park.
With they we use were, not was.
Did you was at school?
Were you at school?
Do not use did with to be; put were before the subject.
