Like, Love, Hate + -ing: Talking About Preferences

We use like, love and hate to say what we enjoy or do not enjoy. After these verbs, we often use another verb ending in -ing, for example I like swimming. This is very common in everyday English.

  1. After like, love and hate, use the -ing form of the next verb: I love reading.
  2. The subject does not change the verb: She likes, he loves, they hate — add -s only to like, love or hate with he/she/it.
  3. Most verbs add -ing: play → playing, read → reading, cook → cooking.
  4. Verbs ending in -e drop the e: make → making, dance → dancing.
  5. Short verbs ending in one vowel + one consonant double the consonant: run → running, swim → swimming.
  6. Use don't / doesn't like for a negative: I don't like cooking, She doesn't like running.

Examples

  • I like listening to music.I like listening to music.
  • She loves cooking dinner for her family.She loves cooking dinner for her family.
  • They hate waiting for the bus.They hate waiting for the bus.
  • He doesn't like swimming in cold water.He doesn't like swimming in cold water.
  • We love watching films at the weekend.We love watching films at the weekend.
  • My brother hates cleaning his room.My brother hates cleaning his room.

Typical mistakes

I like swim.
I like swimming.
After like, love and hate, use the -ing form, not the base verb.
She love reading.
She loves reading.
With he/she/it, add -s to the main verb: loves.
He likes runing.
He likes running.
Run ends in one vowel + one consonant, so double the n: running.

Check yourself

  1. I like ___ books in the evening.

  2. My sister ___ cooking.

  3. They hate ___ early.

  4. He doesn't like ___ the piano.

  5. We love ___ photos of animals.
