Past Continuous: I Was Watching TV (A2 English)

The past continuous describes an action that was in progress at a time in the past. We often use it to show what was happening when something else happened. We form it with was or were + the -ing form of the verb.

  1. Use was with I, he, she, it: I was watching TV.
  2. Use were with you, we, they: They were sleeping.
  3. Add -ing to the verb: watch → watching, read → reading.
  4. For negatives, put not after was/were: She wasn't listening.
  5. For questions, put was/were before the subject: Were you working at 8pm?
  6. Use it for an action in progress in the past, often with while, and for the longer action before a short past simple event: I was cooking when the phone rang.

Examples

  • At 7 o'clock I was watching TV.At seven o'clock I was watching television.
  • She was reading a book when I called her.She was reading a book at the moment I called her.
  • We were playing football all afternoon.We were playing football for the whole afternoon.
  • They weren't listening to the teacher.They were not paying attention to the teacher.
  • Was it raining when you left the house?Was it raining at the time you went out of the house?
  • While my mother was cooking, I was doing my homework.While my mother was cooking, I was doing my homework.

Typical mistakes

I was watch TV yesterday.
I was watching TV yesterday.
After was/were we need the -ing form.
They was playing tennis.
They were playing tennis.
Use were with they.
He was not go to school.
He was not going to school.
Use was/were + -ing in negative sentences.
