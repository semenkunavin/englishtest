Much, Many and A Lot Of: English Quantifiers (A1)

We use much, many and a lot of to talk about the amount or number of something. Many goes with countable nouns, much goes with uncountable nouns, and a lot of works with both. This page shows the simple rules and the most common mistakes.

  1. Use many with countable nouns in the plural: many books, many friends, many cars.
  2. Use much with uncountable nouns: much water, much time, much money.
  3. Use a lot of with both countable and uncountable nouns: a lot of books, a lot of water.
  4. Much and many are mostly used in questions and negative sentences: How many eggs? / I don't have much time.
  5. In positive sentences, a lot of is more natural than much or many: I have a lot of homework, not I have much homework.
  6. Do not use much or many alone in a short positive answer; say a lot: Yes, I have a lot.

Examples

  • How many students are in your class?How many students are in your class?
  • There isn't much milk in the fridge.There isn't much milk in the fridge.
  • She has a lot of friends at school.She has a lot of friends at school.
  • We don't have much time this morning.We don't have much time this morning.
  • I drink a lot of water every day.I drink a lot of water every day.
  • Are there many shops near your house?Are there many shops near your house?

Typical mistakes

I have much books.
I have many books.
Books is countable, so use many, not much.
There are a lot of water in the bottle.
There is a lot of water in the bottle.
Water is uncountable, so the verb is is, not are.
I have many money in my bag.
I have a lot of money in my bag.
Money is uncountable, so many is wrong; use a lot of.

  2. There isn't ___ sugar in my coffee.

  3. He has ___ toys in his bedroom.

  4. I don't have ___ time today.

  5. We see ___ birds in the park every morning.
