So and Such: How to Use Them Correctly

We use so and such to make the meaning of an adjective or noun stronger. So comes before an adjective or adverb, and such comes before a noun or a noun phrase. Both words mean "very" or "really" in these sentences.

  1. Use so + adjective: so tired, so expensive.
  2. Use so + adverb: so quickly, so well.
  3. Use such + (adjective) + noun: such a nice day, such terrible weather.
  4. With singular countable nouns use such a/an + adjective + noun: such a good idea.
  5. Do not use so directly before a noun: say such a big house, not so a big house.
  6. Use so ... that and such ... that to show a result: The film was so boring that I fell asleep.

Examples

  • The coffee is so hot that I can't drink it.The coffee is so hot that I can't drink it.
  • She speaks so quietly that I can't hear her.She speaks so quietly that I can't hear her.
  • It was such a beautiful morning that we walked to work.It was such a beautiful morning that we walked to work.
  • We had such terrible weather on holiday.We had such terrible weather on holiday.
  • He is such a kind person that everyone likes him.He is such a kind person that everyone likes him.
  • I was so surprised that I forgot my answer.I was so surprised that I forgot my answer.

Typical mistakes

She is so a clever student.
She is such a clever student.
Such comes before an adjective + singular noun; so comes only before adjectives.
It was such cold that we stayed inside.
It was so cold that we stayed inside.
Such needs a noun after it; before a plain adjective use so.
He ran such fast that nobody could catch him.
He ran so fast that nobody could catch him.
Before an adverb use so, not such.
