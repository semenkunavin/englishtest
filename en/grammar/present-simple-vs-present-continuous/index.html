Present Simple vs Present Continuous — A1 English

The present simple and the present continuous are two English verb forms for the present. We use the present simple for habits, routines and facts. We use the present continuous for actions happening now or around now.

  1. Use the present simple for habits and routines: I get up at seven every day.
  2. Use the present simple for facts and things that are always true: Water boils at 100 degrees.
  3. Use the present continuous for actions happening now: She is reading a book right now.
  4. Form the present continuous with am/is/are + verb-ing: They are playing football.
  5. Form the present simple with the verb alone; add -s for he, she, it: He works in a bank.
  6. Time words help you choose: every day, usually, often go with the present simple; now, right now, at the moment go with the present continuous.

Examples

  • I usually drink coffee in the morning.I usually drink coffee in the morning.
  • Look! The baby is sleeping.Look! The baby is sleeping.
  • My sister lives in London.My sister lives in London.
  • We are studying English at the moment.We are studying English at the moment.
  • He never eats meat.He never eats meat.
  • Please be quiet — the children are doing their homework now.Please be quiet — the children are doing their homework now.

Typical mistakes

She is liking chocolate.
She likes chocolate.
Verbs of feeling and liking (like, love, want, know) are not usually used in the present continuous.
He go to work every day.
He goes to work every day.
In the present simple, add -s to the verb with he, she and it.
Look! It rains now.
Look! It is raining now.
For an action happening now, use the present continuous: am/is/are + verb-ing.
