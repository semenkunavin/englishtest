Adverbs of Frequency: Always, Usually, Sometimes, Never (A1)

Adverbs of frequency tell you how often something happens. We use always, usually, often, sometimes and never to talk about habits and routines. They answer the question How often?

  1. The order from most to least is: always (100%), usually (about 90%), often (about 70%), sometimes (about 50%), never (0%).
  2. Put the adverb before the main verb: I always drink coffee. / She never eats meat.
  3. With the verb be, put the adverb after it: He is usually late. / They are never sad.
  4. Use never with a positive verb, not not: I never smoke. (not I don't never smoke).
  5. With do/does/did in negative sentences, the adverb goes before the main verb: I don't often go out.
  6. Short answers to How often...? can be just the adverb: How often do you swim? — Sometimes.

Examples

  • I always brush my teeth before bed.I always brush my teeth before bed.
  • She usually walks to work.She usually walks to work.
  • We sometimes eat dinner at a restaurant.We sometimes eat dinner at a restaurant.
  • He is never late for class.He is never late for class.
  • They often watch films on Friday.They often watch films on Friday.
  • My parents are always happy to see me.My parents are always happy to see me.

Typical mistakes

I go always to the gym.
I always go to the gym.
The adverb of frequency goes before the main verb.
She always is tired.
She is always tired.
With the verb be, the adverb goes after it.
I don't never drink coffee.
I never drink coffee.
Use never with a positive verb; two negatives are wrong.

Check yourself

  1. He ___ plays football on Sundays. (100%)

  2. They ___ go to the beach in winter. (0%)

  3. I ___ am tired in the morning.

  4. She ___ eats meat. She is a vegetarian.

  5. We ___ watch TV after dinner, maybe three times a week.
