Verb + -ing or to + infinitive (enjoy doing, want to do)

Some English verbs are followed by an -ing form, and some are followed by to + infinitive. You cannot choose freely: each verb has its own pattern, so you learn which form goes with which verb.

  1. Verbs of liking and not liking usually take -ing: enjoy, like, love, hate, don't mind. Example: I enjoy reading.
  2. Verbs about wants and plans take to + infinitive: want, need, hope, plan, decide, would like. Example: She wants to go home.
  3. After enjoy, finish, stop, mind and suggest, use the -ing form, not to. Example: He finished eating.
  4. After want, need, hope, decide and try, use to + verb, not -ing. Example: We decided to stay.
  5. After can't, don't and other auxiliaries, the verb stays in its base form; the -ing or to comes after the main verb: I can't stop laughing. / I don't want to laugh.
  6. After a preposition, always use -ing: I'm good at cooking. / Thank you for helping.

Examples

  • I enjoy playing tennis at the weekend.I enjoy playing tennis at the weekend.
  • She wants to buy a new phone.She wants to buy a new phone.
  • They finished working at six o'clock.They finished working at six o'clock.
  • We hope to see you soon.We hope to see you soon.
  • He doesn't mind waiting for the bus.He doesn't mind waiting for the bus.
  • My brother decided to study French.My brother decided to study French.

Typical mistakes

I enjoy to read books.
I enjoy reading books.
Enjoy is always followed by the -ing form, never to + infinitive.
She wants going to the cinema.
She wants to go to the cinema.
Want is followed by to + infinitive, not by -ing.
He finished to do his homework.
He finished doing his homework.
Finish takes the -ing form: finish doing.

Check yourself

  1. I enjoy ___ to music in the evening.

  2. They want ___ a house next year.

  3. She finished ___ her report at midnight.

  4. We hope ___ you again soon.

  5. Do you mind ___ the window?
