Present Perfect Continuous (I've been waiting) – B1

The present perfect continuous describes an action that started in the past and is still happening, or has just stopped and has a visible result. We often use it to say how long something has been going on. It connects the past to now: I've been waiting for an hour.

  1. Form: have/has been + verb-ing. Short forms: I've been working, she's been studying.
  2. Use it for actions that started before now and are still in progress: I've been waiting for the bus since 8 o'clock.
  3. Use it for actions that have just stopped and explain a present result: My hands are dirty because I've been fixing my bike.
  4. Ask How long have you been…? and answer with for + a period or since + a starting point.
  5. Negative: haven't/hasn't been + verb-ing – He hasn't been feeling well lately.
  6. Stative verbs (know, want, like, believe) usually do not take the continuous form: say I've known her for years.

Examples

  • I've been waiting for you for twenty minutes.I have been waiting for you for twenty minutes.
  • She's been working at that café since last summer.She has been working at that café since last summer.
  • Why are you so tired? Have you been running?Why are you so tired? Have you been running?
  • They haven't been sleeping well because of the noise.They have not been sleeping well because of the noise.
  • How long have you been learning English?How long have you been learning English?
  • It's been raining all morning, so the streets are wet.It has been raining all morning, so the streets are wet.

Typical mistakes

I have been knowing him for ten years.
I have known him for ten years.
know is a stative verb, so use the present perfect, not the continuous.
She has been work here since May.
She has been working here since May.
After been we need the -ing form of the verb.
I've been waiting for you since two hours.
I've been waiting for you for two hours.
Use for with a period of time and since with a starting point.
