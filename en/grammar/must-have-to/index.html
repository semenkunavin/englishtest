Must and Have To: Rules and Obligations in English (A2)

We use must and have to to talk about things that are necessary or obligatory. The meaning is often the same, but must is more personal and have to is more about rules from other people. Both are followed by the base verb: must go, have to go.

  1. Use must + base verb for strong personal obligation: I must call my mum.
  2. Use have to + base verb for rules and obligations from outside: I have to wear a uniform at work.
  3. Must has one form for all persons: he must, she must, they must. Do not add -s.
  4. Have to changes with the person: he has to, she has to; questions and negatives use do: Do you have to work?, I don't have to work.
  5. For past obligation use had to, not must: I had to leave early.
  6. Negative meanings are different: mustn't = it is forbidden; don't have to = it is not necessary.

Examples

  • You must show your passport at the airport.You must show your passport at the airport.
  • I have to get up early on Mondays.I have to get up early on Mondays.
  • She has to wear a helmet when she rides her bike.She has to wear a helmet when she rides her bike.
  • Do you have to pay for parking here?Do you have to pay for parking here?
  • We had to wait an hour for the bus yesterday.We had to wait an hour for the bus yesterday.
  • You mustn't use your phone in the exam, but you don't have to turn it off.You mustn't use your phone in the exam, but you don't have to turn it off.

Typical mistakes

He musts finish his homework.
He must finish his homework.
Must never takes -s with he/she/it.
She have to work on Saturday.
She has to work on Saturday.
With he/she/it, have to becomes has to.
Yesterday I must go to the doctor.
Yesterday I had to go to the doctor.
For past obligation use had to; must has no past form.
