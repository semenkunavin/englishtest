Adverbs of Manner: Quickly, Well (A2 English)

Adverbs of manner tell us how someone does something: quickly, slowly, well. We often use them after a verb, for example She speaks quickly. Many of them are made by adding -ly to an adjective.

  1. Most adverbs of manner are adjective + -ly: quick → quickly, slow → slowly, careful → carefully.
  2. If an adjective ends in -y, change y to i and add -ly: easy → easily, happy → happily.
  3. Good is an adjective; the adverb is well: He is a good singer, but He sings well.
  4. Fast and hard are the same as adjective and adverb: He works hard. Do not say 'hardly' for this meaning.
  5. Put the adverb after the verb or after the object: He drives carefully. She speaks English well.
  6. Adverbs of manner usually do not go between the verb and its object: say He speaks English well, not He speaks well English.

Examples

  • She speaks English very well.She speaks English very well.
  • Please drive carefully in the rain.Please drive carefully in the rain.
  • He finished the test quickly.He finished the test quickly.
  • My sister sings beautifully.My sister sings beautifully.
  • They worked hard all day.They worked hard all day.
  • The old man walked slowly to the door.The old man walked slowly to the door.

Typical mistakes

She sings very good.
She sings very well.
After a verb we need the adverb well, not the adjective good.
He speaks well English.
He speaks English well.
The adverb comes after the object, not between the verb and its object.
She ran very quick to the bus.
She ran very quickly to the bus.
We need the adverb quickly (with -ly) to describe how she ran.
