Present Perfect with For and Since (A2 English)

We use the present perfect with for and since to talk about something that started in the past and is still true now. For gives a length of time (for three years), and since gives the starting point (since 2020).

  1. Use have/has + past participle before for or since: I have lived here for two years.
  2. Use for with a period of time: for two days, for six months, for a long time.
  3. Use since with a point in time: since Monday, since 2019, since I was ten.
  4. With since, the time can be a date, a day, a month, a year, or a past event: since last summer, since Christmas.
  5. Questions: How long have you known her? Answer: For five years. or Since 2021.
  6. Do not use for with a date and do not use since with a length of time: for two weeks, since June.

Examples

  • I have studied English for three years.I have studied English for three years.
  • She has worked here since 2021.She has worked here since 2021.
  • We have known each other for a long time.We have known each other for a long time.
  • He has had that bike since he was twelve.He has had that bike since he was twelve.
  • They have lived in London for six months.They have lived in London for six months.
  • How long have you been a teacher? Since 2018.How long have you been a teacher? Since 2018.

Typical mistakes

I have lived here since two years.
I have lived here for two years.
Two years is a length of time, so we use for, not since.
She has worked here for 2021.
She has worked here since 2021.
2021 is a starting point, so we use since.
I am in this class for three weeks.
I have been in this class for three weeks.
Use the present perfect (have been), not the present simple, with for and since.
