A few and a little: Countable and Uncountable Nouns (A2)

We use a few and a little to talk about small quantities. A few goes with plural countable nouns, and a little goes with uncountable nouns. Both have a positive meaning: the quantity is small, but it is enough.

  1. Use a few + plural countable noun: a few books, a few friends.
  2. Use a little + uncountable noun: a little water, a little time.
  3. Never use a little with plural nouns: say a few apples, not a little apples.
  4. Never use a few with uncountable nouns: say a little money, not a few money.
  5. A few and a little are positive: there is some, and it is enough.
  6. Without a, few and little are negative: few friends means almost no friends, little time means almost no time.

Examples

  • I have a few questions about the homework.I have a few questions about the homework.
  • She needs a little sugar for the cake.She needs a little sugar for the cake.
  • There are a few cars in the car park.There are a few cars in the car park.
  • Can I have a little milk in my coffee, please?Can I have a little milk in my coffee, please?
  • We spent a few days in London last summer.We spent a few days in London last summer.
  • He speaks a little Spanish, so he can order food.He speaks a little Spanish, so he can order food.

Typical mistakes

I have a few money in my bag.
I have a little money in my bag.
Money is uncountable, so it takes a little.
There are a little people in the shop.
There are a few people in the shop.
People is a plural countable noun, so use a few.
Can you give me a few water?
Can you give me a little water?
Water is uncountable, so use a little.
