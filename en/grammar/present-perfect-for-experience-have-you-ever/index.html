Present Perfect for Experience: Have You Ever...?

We use the present perfect to talk about experiences in your life, when the exact time is not important. Questions with Have you ever...? ask if something happened at any time before now. We often answer with I have or I haven't, and we can add details with the past simple.

  1. Form the question with Have/Has + subject + ever + past participle: Have you ever been to London?
  2. Answer with Yes, I have or No, I haven't. For he/she/it, use Has he/she/it ever...?
  3. Use ever in questions and never in negative meaning: I have never eaten sushi.
  4. Never already makes the sentence negative, so do not add not: say I have never seen it, not I haven't never seen it.
  5. Use the past participle (the third form): be - was/were - been, go - went - gone/been, see - saw - seen.
  6. Do not use a finished past time such as yesterday or last year with this form; use the past simple instead: I saw it last year.

Examples

  • Have you ever ridden a horse?Have you ever ridden a horse?
  • I have never been to Japan.I have never been to Japan.
  • She has visited Paris three times.She has visited Paris three times.
  • We have tried Vietnamese food before.We have tried Vietnamese food before.
  • Has he ever worked in another country?Has he ever worked in another country?
  • They haven't seen the new film yet.They haven't seen the new film yet.

Typical mistakes

I have ever been to Rome.
I have been to Rome.
Ever is used in questions, not in positive statements.
I haven't never eaten sushi.
I have never eaten sushi.
Never is already negative, so do not add not.
Have you ever went to Spain?
Have you ever been to Spain?
Use the past participle been after have, not the past simple went.
