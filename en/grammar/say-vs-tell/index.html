Say vs Tell: How to Use Them Correctly in English

Both say and tell mean 'to communicate with words', but they are used in different patterns. The main difference is that tell usually needs a person after it, while say does not.

  1. Use tell + person: tell somebody something. Example: She told me the news.
  2. Use say without a person: say something. Example: He said goodbye.
  3. If you want a person with say, use to: say something to somebody. Example: He said hello to me.
  4. Use tell for instructions and stories: tell a story, tell a lie, tell the truth, tell somebody to do something.
  5. Use say in direct speech: She said, 'I'm tired.' Never use tell with these exact words alone.
  6. Both verbs are irregular: say – said – said, tell – told – told.

Examples

  • He told me his name.He gave me his name using words.
  • She said she was busy.She communicated that she was busy, with no person after the verb.
  • I said goodbye to my teacher.I used the words 'goodbye' when speaking to my teacher.
  • Can you tell me the time, please?Can you give me the time information?
  • The teacher told us to open our books.The teacher gave us an instruction.
  • What did you say?What words did you speak?

Typical mistakes

She said me the truth.
She told me the truth.
Say cannot take a person directly; use tell + person.
He told that he was late.
He said that he was late.
Without a person after it, use say, not tell.
I told to him the answer.
I told him the answer.
Tell is followed directly by the person, with no to.

Check yourself

  1. She ___ me she would come later.

  2. He ___ that the film was boring.

  3. Please ___ the truth.

  4. I ___ hello to her this morning.

  5. The doctor ___ me to rest for a week.
