This, That, These, Those: Demonstratives in English (A1)

We use this, that, these and those to point to things. The choice depends on two things: is the thing near or far, and is it one thing or more than one? These words are called demonstratives.

  1. This = one thing near you. Example: this book (in my hand).
  2. That = one thing far from you. Example: that car (across the street).
  3. These = two or more things near you. Example: these shoes (at my feet).
  4. Those = two or more things far from you. Example: those birds (in the sky).
  5. Use this/that with singular nouns and these/those with plural nouns. Match the verb: this is, these are.
  6. You can use them alone when the thing is clear: What is this? — This is my phone.

Examples

  • This is my new phone.The phone is here, in my hand.
  • That is my school over there.The school is far away from us.
  • These apples are very sweet.The apples are here, near the speaker.
  • Those are my friends near the gate.The friends are over there, at a distance.
  • Is this your bag?Asking about one thing that is near.
  • What are those in the tree?Asking about several things far away.

Typical mistakes

These is my friends.
These are my friends.
These is plural, so the verb must be are.
This books are new.
These books are new.
Books is plural, so use these, not this.
That are my car.
That is my car.
That is singular, so the verb must be is.

Check yourself

  1. ___ is my coffee cup. It is right here.

  2. Look at ___ stars in the sky!

  3. ___ are my keys in my hand.

  4. Can you see ___ house across the river?

  5. ___ shoes next to me are dirty.
