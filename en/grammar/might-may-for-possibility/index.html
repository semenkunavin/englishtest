Might / May for Possibility — Simple A2 English Guide

We use may and might to say that something is possible, but we are not sure. They are followed by a verb in the base form, with no to and no -s.

  1. Use may + verb or might + verb to say that something is possible: It may rain. / It might rain.
  2. After may and might, use the base form of the verb: She might come, not She might comes.
  3. For the negative, use may not or might not (no don't): He might not be at home.
  4. To ask about possibility, use Do you think you might…? or use Could…?; May you…? is not used for possibility.
  5. May and might are very similar. Might often sounds a little less sure: I might go, but I'm not sure.
  6. May is also used for permission: May I sit here? This is a different use, not possibility.

Examples

  • It may rain this afternoon, so take an umbrella.It is possible that it will rain this afternoon.
  • She might be late because the bus is slow.It is possible that she will be late.
  • We may not have time to visit the museum.It is possible that we will not have time.
  • My brother might come to dinner on Sunday.It is possible that my brother will come to dinner.
  • There may be a problem with the computer.It is possible that there is a problem.
  • They might not know about the meeting.It is possible that they do not know about the meeting.

Typical mistakes

She might comes tomorrow.
She might come tomorrow.
After might, use the base form of the verb, not the -s form.
It may to snow tonight.
It may snow tonight.
Do not put to between may and the verb.
He might doesn't be at home.
He might not be at home.
For the negative, use might not + verb; do not use doesn't.

Check yourself

  1. Look at those clouds. It ___ rain soon.

  2. I'm not sure, but she ___ be in the office.

  3. Take your phone. We ___ need it later.

  4. He ___ come to the party — he is very busy.

  5. There ___ a train at 6 p.m. I am not sure.
