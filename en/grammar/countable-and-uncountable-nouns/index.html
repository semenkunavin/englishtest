Countable and Uncountable Nouns in English (A1)

Some English nouns are countable and some are uncountable. Countable nouns have a singular and a plural form and take a/an or numbers. Uncountable nouns have no plural and we do not use a/an with them.

  1. Countable nouns have a plural: one book – two books, a chair – three chairs.
  2. Use a/an with singular countable nouns: a pen, an apple. Do not use a/an with plural countable nouns: books, not a books.
  3. Uncountable nouns have no plural: water, milk, rice, money, information. Do not use a/an or numbers with them.
  4. Use some, much and a lot of with uncountable nouns: some water, much time, a lot of money.
  5. Use many and a few with plural countable nouns: many cars, a few apples.
  6. To count an uncountable noun, use a container or unit: a glass of water, a cup of coffee, a piece of information, two kilos of rice.

Examples

  • I have two brothers and one sister.I have two brothers and one sister.
  • There is some milk in the fridge.There is some milk in the fridge.
  • She needs a lot of information about the course.She needs a lot of information about the course.
  • How many eggs do you want?How many eggs do you want?
  • Can I have a glass of water, please?Can I have a glass of water, please?
  • We don't have much time this morning.We don't have much time this morning.

Typical mistakes

I need some informations.
I need some information.
Information is uncountable and has no plural form.
She bought a bread and two milks.
She bought a loaf of bread and two bottles of milk.
Bread and milk are uncountable, so use a unit word to count them.
There are many furniture in the room.
There is a lot of furniture in the room.
Furniture is uncountable: use a lot of and a singular verb.
