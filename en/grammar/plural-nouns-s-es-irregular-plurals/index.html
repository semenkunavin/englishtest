Plural Nouns: -s, -es and Irregular Plurals (A1)

A plural noun means more than one person, thing or animal. Most English nouns add -s or -es, but some common nouns change in a special way. We use plurals every day: two books, three boxes, many children.

  1. Add -s to most nouns: book → books, car → cars, dog → dogs.
  2. Add -es after -s, -ss, -sh, -ch, -x: bus → buses, box → boxes, watch → watches.
  3. A consonant + -y changes to -ies: city → cities, baby → babies. But a vowel + -y just adds -s: boy → boys.
  4. Some nouns are irregular: man → men, woman → women, child → children, foot → feet, tooth → teeth, mouse → mice.
  5. Some nouns do not change: one sheep → two sheep, one fish → two fish.
  6. Use a or an with one thing, and no article with a plural: a book, books.

Examples

  • I have two brothers and one sister.I have two brothers and one sister.
  • There are three buses in front of the school.There are three buses in front of the school.
  • The children are playing in the park.The children are playing in the park.
  • My feet are cold.My feet are cold.
  • She likes apples and oranges.She likes apples and oranges.
  • Those women are teachers.Those women are teachers.

Typical mistakes

I have two childs.
I have two children.
Child is irregular: the plural is children, not childs.
There are three boxs on the table.
There are three boxes on the table.
After -x we add -es: box → boxes.
My foots hurt.
My feet hurt.
Foot is irregular: the plural is feet.
