Too and Enough in English: Rules and Examples

We use too and enough to say if something is the right amount or not. Too means 'more than we want or need', and enough means 'the right amount, as much as we need'. These words are very common in everyday English.

  1. Use too before an adjective or adverb: too hot, too fast, too expensive.
  2. Use too before a noun with much or many: too much sugar, too many people.
  3. Use enough after an adjective or adverb: old enough, fast enough.
  4. Use enough before a noun: enough money, enough time.
  5. We often use to + verb after both words: too tired to work, old enough to drive.
  6. Do not use too to mean 'very'. Too always shows a problem or a negative idea.

Examples

  • This coffee is too hot to drink.This coffee is too hot to drink.
  • She is not old enough to drive a car.She is not old enough to drive a car.
  • We don't have enough time to finish the test.We don't have enough time to finish the test.
  • There are too many cars in the city centre.There are too many cars in the city centre.
  • The room was big enough for all of us.The room was big enough for all of us.
  • He speaks too quickly, so I can't understand him.He speaks too quickly, so I can't understand him.

Typical mistakes

It is enough warm to go outside.
It is warm enough to go outside.
Enough comes after an adjective, not before it.
I am too tired that I can't walk.
I am too tired to walk.
After too + adjective, use to + verb, not that.
I have too money for the ticket.
I have enough money for the ticket.
Before a noun we say enough money; too before a noun needs much or many.
