Imperatives in English: Open the Door, Don't Run

The imperative is the form we use to tell someone to do something or not to do something. We use it for orders, instructions, advice, and requests. It is very common in everyday English: Open the door. Don't run.

  1. Use the base verb with no subject: Open the door. Sit down.
  2. For a negative order, put don't before the verb: Don't run. Don't touch it.
  3. Add please to be polite: Please sit down. Don't open the window, please.
  4. The imperative is the same for one person or many people: Come in. (to one person or to a group)
  5. You can use let's + verb to include yourself: Let's go. Let's not wait.
  6. For instructions, use short imperatives one after another: Open the box. Take the paper out. Close it.

Examples

  • Open the door, please.Please open the door.
  • Don't run in the classroom.Do not run in the classroom.
  • Sit down and listen.Sit down and listen to me.
  • Don't touch the hot pan.Do not touch the hot pan.
  • Let's go home now.Let us go home now.
  • Please don't be late.Please do not be late.

Typical mistakes

You open the door.
Open the door.
The imperative has no subject like 'you'.
Not run.
Don't run.
Negative imperatives need 'don't' before the verb.
Don't to run.
Don't run.
Use the base verb after 'don't', without 'to'.
