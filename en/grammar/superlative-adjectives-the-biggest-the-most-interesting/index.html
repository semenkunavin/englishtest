Superlative Adjectives: the Biggest, the Most Interesting

We use superlative adjectives to compare three or more things and say which one is at the top: the biggest, the fastest, the most interesting. Short adjectives take -est, longer adjectives take the most. Superlatives almost always have the before them.

  1. Short adjectives (1 syllable): add -est — tall → the tallest, fast → the fastest.
  2. Adjectives ending in -e: add -st — large → the largest, nice → the nicest.
  3. Ending in consonant + -y: change y to i and add -est — happy → the happiest.
  4. Short adjectives ending in consonant-vowel-consonant double the last letter — big → the biggest, hot → the hottest.
  5. Longer adjectives (2+ syllables): use the most + adjective — the most interesting, the most expensive, the most beautiful.
  6. Irregular forms: good → the best, bad → the worst, far → the furthest. Always use the before the superlative.

Examples

  • Mount Everest is the highest mountain in the world.Mount Everest is the highest mountain in the world.
  • This is the biggest pizza on the menu.This is the biggest pizza on the menu.
  • She is the most interesting person in the room.She is the most interesting person in the room.
  • December is the busiest month for shops.December is the busiest month for shops.
  • That was the best film I saw last year.That was the best film I saw last year.
  • Our trip to the coast was the most expensive holiday of all.Our trip to the coast was the most expensive holiday of all.

Typical mistakes

He is the most tall in his class.
He is the tallest in his class.
Short adjectives take -est, not the most.
This is biggest room in the house.
This is the biggest room in the house.
A superlative needs the before it.
She is more happy today than yesterday, the happier of the two.
She is the happiest person in the family.
Change y to i: happy → the happiest. Don't use more with -est forms.
