Passive Voice: Present and Past (B1 English)

The passive voice lets us focus on the action or the person or thing affected, not on who does it. We use it a lot in news, rules, instructions and when the doer is unknown or obvious. At B1 you need the present simple passive and the past simple passive.

  1. The passive is be + past participle: English is spoken here. / The letter was sent yesterday.
  2. Present simple passive: am / is / are + past participle: The rooms are cleaned every day.
  3. Past simple passive: was / were + past participle: The car was repaired last week.
  4. To say who does the action, use by + person: The novel was written by Orwell.
  5. Regular past participles end in -ed (opened, cleaned); irregular verbs have a third form (written, made, sold).
  6. The object of the active sentence becomes the subject: They built the bridge → The bridge was built.

Examples

  • English is spoken in many countries.Present simple passive with a general fact.
  • These rooms are cleaned every morning.Present simple passive with a repeated action.
  • The window was broken last night.Past simple passive with a finished action.
  • Millions of phones are made in China.Plural subject with are + past participle.
  • The cake was eaten by the children.Past simple passive with by + doer.
  • Hamlet was written by Shakespeare.Past simple passive with an irregular participle.

Typical mistakes

The house was build in 1990.
The house was built in 1990.
Use the past participle built, not the base form build.
The offices is cleaned every day.
The offices are cleaned every day.
A plural subject takes are in the present passive.
The letter was sent by the letter.
The letter was sent by my brother.
Use by + the person who does the action, not the object.

Check yourself

  1. The book ___ by a famous writer in 1950.

  2. Many emails ___ every day in this office.

  3. The thieves ___ by the police last night.

  4. This song ___ all over the world.
