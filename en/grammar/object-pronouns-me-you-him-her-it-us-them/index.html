Object Pronouns in English: me, you, him, her, it, us, them

Object pronouns replace a noun that receives the action of a verb or follows a preposition. They are me, you, him, her, it, us, them. We use them after verbs like like, see, help, and after prepositions like for, with, to.

  1. Use me, you, him, her, it, us, them after a verb: I see him, She likes us.
  2. you and it are the same for one person or thing and for many: I know you, I like it, I need them (things = them).
  3. Use him for a man or boy, and her for a woman or girl: Call him, Call her.
  4. Use it for one thing or animal: I have a dog. I love it.
  5. After a preposition, use the object pronoun: for me, with her, to them.
  6. me and us are for the speaker alone and the speaker with others: Help me, Help us.

Examples

  • Please help me with this bag.Please help me with this bag.
  • I like your new shoes. I love them.I like your new shoes. I love them.
  • We see Tom every day. We see him at school.We see Tom every day. We see him at school.
  • This is for Anna. Give it to her.This is for Anna. Give it to her.
  • The coffee is cold. I don't want it.The coffee is cold. I don't want it.
  • Come with us to the park!Come with us to the park!

Typical mistakes

I see he every day.
I see him every day.
After a verb we need the object form him, not the subject form he.
This present is for I.
This present is for me.
After a preposition we use me, not I.
She likes them dog.
She likes that dog. / She likes it.
them is a pronoun; do not put it before a noun. Use it for one dog.

  2. I like Anna. I like ___ a lot.

  3. This book is great. I read ___ every night.

  4. We are ready. Come to the party with ___.

  5. Tom and Kate are nice. I know ___ well.
