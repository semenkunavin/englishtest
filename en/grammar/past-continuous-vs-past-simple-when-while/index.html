Past Continuous vs Past Simple (When / While) for A2

We use the past simple for a short, finished action. We use the past continuous for an action that was happening around a time in the past. We often join the two with when and while.

  1. The past continuous shows a longer action in progress: was/were + verb-ing. Example: I was cooking dinner.
  2. The past simple shows a short action that interrupts: I was cooking dinner when the phone rang.
  3. Use when before the short past simple action: when he arrived, when it started to rain.
  4. Use while before the longer past continuous action: while I was walking home, while they were playing.
  5. After when you can also use the past continuous for a longer background action: When I was young, I lived in Rome.
  6. Do not use while with a short past simple action: say when the phone rang, not while the phone rang.

Examples

  • I was taking a shower when the doorbell rang.I was taking a shower when the doorbell rang.
  • While she was studying, her brother was watching TV.While she was studying, her brother was watching TV.
  • We were driving home when we saw an accident.We were driving home when we saw an accident.
  • He fell asleep while he was reading a book.He fell asleep while he was reading a book.
  • What were you doing when I called you?What were you doing when I called you?
  • They were playing football when it started to rain.They were playing football when it started to rain.

Typical mistakes

While the phone rang, I was cooking.
The phone rang while I was cooking.
While goes with the longer past continuous action, not the short past simple one.
I cooked when the phone rang.
I was cooking when the phone rang.
The longer background action needs the past continuous: was cooking.
She was studied when I arrived.
She was studying when I arrived.
After was/were we use the -ing form, not the past simple.
