Present Simple: Negatives and Questions with Do / Does

We use the present simple to talk about habits, routines and facts. To make negatives and questions, we use the helping verbs do and does. This page shows you how to build these sentences correctly.

  1. Use don't (= do not) with I, you, we, they: I don't like coffee.
  2. Use doesn't (= does not) with he, she, it: She doesn't work here.
  3. Start questions with Do + I/you/we/they: Do you speak English?
  4. Start questions with Does + he/she/it: Does he live in London?
  5. After doesn't and Does, use the base verb without -s: Does she like tea? (not likes).
  6. Short answers: Yes, I do. / No, I don't. / Yes, she does. / No, she doesn't.

Examples

  • I don't eat meat.I don't eat meat.
  • He doesn't drive to work.He doesn't drive to work.
  • Do they play football on Sundays?Do they play football on Sundays?
  • Does your sister study French?Does your sister study French?
  • We don't watch TV in the morning.We don't watch TV in the morning.
  • Does it rain a lot here?Does it rain a lot here?

Typical mistakes

She don't like fish.
She doesn't like fish.
With she we use doesn't, not don't.
Does he works here?
Does he work here?
After Does we use the base verb without -s.
I not understand.
I don't understand.
Negatives need the helping verb don't before the main verb.

Check yourself

  1. ___ you like tea?

  2. She ___ live in Paris.

  3. ___ he play tennis?

  4. They ___ work on Saturdays.

  5. ___ your parents speak English?
