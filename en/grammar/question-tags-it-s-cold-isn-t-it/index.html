Question Tags in English: It's Cold, Isn't It?

A question tag is a short question added to the end of a statement, like isn't it? in It's cold, isn't it? We use it to check information, ask for agreement, or keep a conversation going. In English, the tag matches the verb and subject of the main sentence.

  1. A positive statement takes a negative tag: You are tired, aren't you?
  2. A negative statement takes a positive tag: You aren't tired, are you?
  3. The tag repeats the auxiliary verb of the main sentence: She can swim, can't she? / They have left, haven't they?
  4. Use do/does/did in the tag when the main verb has no auxiliary: You like coffee, don't you? / He called, didn't he?
  5. The tag always uses a pronoun, never a noun: Your brother is here, isn't he? (not isn't your brother?)
  6. Special forms: I am ..., aren't I?, Let's ..., shall we?, and positive will/can tags after imperatives: Sit down, won't you?
  7. Falling intonation means you expect agreement; rising intonation means it is a real question and you are not sure.

Examples

  • It's cold today, isn't it?It's cold today, isn't it? (You expect the other person to agree.)
  • You don't eat meat, do you?You don't eat meat, do you? (Negative statement, positive tag.)
  • She works in a bank, doesn't she?She works in a bank, doesn't she? (Present simple, so we use doesn't.)
  • They went to Rome last year, didn't they?They went to Rome last year, didn't they? (Past simple, so we use didn't.)
  • You haven't finished yet, have you?You haven't finished yet, have you? (Rising intonation makes it a real question.)
  • Let's take a break, shall we?Let's take a break, shall we? (Fixed form after Let's.)

Typical mistakes

It's cold, isn't it cold?
It's cold, isn't it?
The tag uses a pronoun, not the whole phrase.
You like coffee, don't you like it?
You like coffee, don't you?
After don't we use only the pronoun, not the verb and object again.
She isn't coming, isn't she?
She isn't coming, is she?
A negative statement needs a positive tag.
