Have got / Has got: English Grammar for Beginners (A1)

We use have got and has got to talk about things we own, people we know, and how we look. It is very common in everyday spoken English. The meaning is the same as have and has.

  1. Use have got with I, you, we, they: I have got a bike.
  2. Use has got with he, she, it: She has got a dog.
  3. Negative: haven't got / hasn't got: He hasn't got a car.
  4. Questions: put have or has first: Have you got a pen? / Has she got a brother?
  5. Short answers: Yes, I have. / No, she hasn't. Do not repeat got.
  6. Do not use do/does with have got: say Have you got...?, not Do you have got...?.

Examples

  • I have got two sisters.I have got two sisters.
  • She has got long hair.She has got long hair.
  • We haven't got a garden.We haven't got a garden.
  • He hasn't got a phone.He hasn't got a phone.
  • Have you got a minute?Have you got a minute?
  • Has your brother got a car?Has your brother got a car?

Typical mistakes

She have got a cat.
She has got a cat.
With he, she, it we use has got.
Do you have got a pen?
Have you got a pen?
Do not use do with have got; put have first.
Yes, she has got.
Yes, she has.
In short answers we do not repeat got.

Check yourself

  1. He ___ got a new bike.

  2. They ___ got any children.

  3. ___ you got a dictionary?

  4. I have got a dog. ___ got a cat, too.

  5. ___ your sister got a job?
