Prepositions of Time: In, On, At (A1 English)

Prepositions of time are small words that come before a time expression: in, on and at. We use them to say when something happens — a month, a day, a clock time and more. The rule is simple: in for long periods, on for days and dates, at for exact times.

  1. Use in with months, years, seasons and parts of the day: in July, in 2024, in winter, in the morning.
  2. Use on with days and dates: on Monday, on 5 May, on my birthday, on Friday morning.
  3. Use at with clock times and exact points: at 7 o'clock, at noon, at midnight, at lunchtime.
  4. Use at in fixed phrases: at night, at the weekend, at Christmas, at the moment.
  5. Remember the order from big to small: in (long) → on (a day) → at (a time).
  6. We often leave the preposition out with next, last, this and every: next week, last Monday, every day.

Examples

  • My birthday is in September.My birthday is in September.
  • We have English class on Tuesday.We have English class on Tuesday.
  • The film starts at 8 o'clock.The film starts at 8 o'clock.
  • She was born in 2010.She was born in 2010.
  • I get up early in the morning.I get up early in the morning.
  • We meet at the weekend.We meet at the weekend.

Typical mistakes

I go to school in Monday.
I go to school on Monday.
Days of the week take on, not in.
The party starts on 9 o'clock.
The party starts at 9 o'clock.
Clock times take at, not on.
My holiday is on July.
My holiday is in July.
Months take in, not on.
