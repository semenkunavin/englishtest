Relative Clauses: Who, Which, That (A2 English)

A relative clause gives more information about a person or a thing. We join two sentences with who, which or that. For example: "I know a girl. She speaks French" becomes "I know a girl who speaks French".

  1. Use who for people: the man who lives next door.
  2. Use which for things and animals: the book which is on the table.
  3. You can use that for people and things: the woman that called you, the car that is red.
  4. The relative clause comes after the noun it describes.
  5. Do not repeat the subject pronoun: say "the boy who is here", not "the boy who he is here".
  6. The verb in the clause agrees with the noun before it: a girl who sings, two girls who sing.

Examples

  • I have a friend who works in a hospital.I have a friend who works in a hospital.
  • This is the bus which goes to the airport.This is the bus which goes to the airport.
  • The dog that lives next door is very friendly.The dog that lives next door is very friendly.
  • We met a woman who speaks five languages.We met a woman who speaks five languages.
  • Where is the phone that I bought yesterday?Where is the phone that I bought yesterday?
  • People who live in big cities often use bikes.People who live in big cities often use bikes.

Typical mistakes

I know a man which works in a bank.
I know a man who works in a bank.
For people we use who (or that), not which.
The boy who he is my brother is ten.
The boy who is my brother is ten.
Do not repeat the subject with he or it after who or which.
The book who is on the table is mine.
The book which is on the table is mine.
Who is only for people; for things we use which or that.

Check yourself

  1. She is the teacher ___ helps me with English.

  2. This is a film ___ is very funny.

  3. I like people ___ are friendly.

  4. Where is the bag ___ I put here?

  5. He has a sister ___ lives in London.
