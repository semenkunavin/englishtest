Prepositions of Place: in, on, at, under, next to

Prepositions of place tell us where something or someone is. We use in, on, at, under and next to to answer the question "Where?". These words are short, but they are very common in everyday English.

  1. Use in when something is inside a place or a container: in the box, in the kitchen, in London.
  2. Use on when something touches a surface: on the table, on the wall, on the floor.
  3. Use at for a point or a place you visit: at the door, at school, at the bus stop.
  4. Use under when something is below another thing: under the bed, under the chair.
  5. Use next to when something is beside another thing: next to the window, next to me.
  6. After these prepositions, use a noun with the or a/an, or a name: on the desk, in a bag, at home.

Examples

  • The keys are in my bag.The keys are in my bag.
  • Your phone is on the table.Your phone is on the table.
  • She is at the bus stop.She is at the bus stop.
  • The cat is under the chair.The cat is under the chair.
  • The lamp is next to the bed.The lamp is next to the bed.
  • We are at home now.We are at home now.

Typical mistakes

The book is in the table.
The book is on the table.
The book touches the top surface of the table, so we use on, not in.
She is in school today.
She is at school today.
For a place she visits for a purpose, we usually say at school.
The cat is next the chair.
The cat is next to the chair.
Next needs to before a noun: next to the chair.
