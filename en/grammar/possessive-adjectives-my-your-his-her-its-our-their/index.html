Possessive Adjectives: my, your, his, her, its, our, their

Possessive adjectives are short words that show who owns or has something. We put them before a noun: my book, her car. They never change for singular or plural nouns.

  1. Use my for the speaker, your for the person you speak to, and our for a group that includes the speaker.
  2. Use his for one male person, her for one female person, and their for more than one person.
  3. Use its for a thing or an animal: the dog and its food. Do not write it's here; it's means 'it is'.
  4. The form never changes: we say my books, his children, their house. There is no plural form like 'mys'.
  5. Always put a noun after the word: her bag, not only 'her bag is'. These words go before the noun, not after it.
  6. Use their with a singular person when you do not know if it is a man or a woman: Someone left their phone.

Examples

  • This is my sister and her name is Emma.This is my sister and her name is Emma.
  • Is that your car outside?Is that your car outside?
  • Tom loves his new bike.Tom loves his new bike.
  • We visit our grandparents every Sunday.We visit our grandparents every Sunday.
  • The cat is eating its food.The cat is eating its food.
  • The students put their books on the desk.The students put their books on the desk.

Typical mistakes

She likes him car.
She likes his car.
him is an object pronoun; before a noun we need the possessive adjective his.
The dog lost it's bone.
The dog lost its bone.
it's means 'it is'; the possessive form is its.
They love they house.
They love their house.
they is a subject pronoun; use their before a noun.
