Present Perfect with just, already and yet (A2 English)

We use the present perfect with just, already and yet to talk about recent actions and things we expect to happen. They show how recent an action is or whether it is done. The form is have/has + just/already/yet + past participle.

  1. Use just for an action that happened a very short time ago: I've just finished my homework.
  2. Just goes between have/has and the past participle: She has just left.
  3. Use already when something happened sooner than expected: We've already eaten.
  4. Already also goes between have/has and the past participle: He has already seen that film.
  5. Use yet in questions and negatives to ask if something is done or to say it is not done: Have you finished yet? / I haven't finished yet.
  6. Yet usually goes at the end of the sentence: They haven't arrived yet.

Examples

  • I've just got home, so I'm still wet from the rain.I have just arrived home a moment ago.
  • Don't buy milk — we've already got two bottles.We have already bought milk, sooner than expected.
  • Have you cleaned your room yet?I am asking if the cleaning is done now.
  • She hasn't called me back yet.The call has not happened up to now, but it may happen.
  • He has just started his new job this week.The job started a very short time ago.
  • We've already seen that film, so let's watch something else.We saw the film before now.

Typical mistakes

I have already ate lunch.
I have already eaten lunch.
After have/has we need the past participle (eaten), not the past simple (ate).
I have finished just my homework.
I have just finished my homework.
Just goes between have/has and the past participle, not after the object.
I haven't finished already.
I haven't finished yet.
In negatives we use yet, not already.
