Modals of Deduction: Must, Might, Can't (B1)

Modals of deduction are words we use to say how sure we are about something. We use must when we are almost sure something is true, might when we think it is possible, and can't when we are sure it is impossible. We use them to guess about the present, for example when we look at a photo or hear a noise.

  1. Use must + verb when the evidence is strong and you are almost sure: You must be tired.
  2. Use might (or may / could) + verb when something is possible but not certain: She might be at work.
  3. Use can't + verb when you are sure something is impossible: That can't be true.
  4. Use must not / mustn't for prohibition, not for deduction. For a negative deduction, use can't.
  5. After every modal, use the base verb with no to and no -s: He must know, not He must knows.
  6. To talk about the past, use modal + have + past participle: They must have left, She can't have seen us.

Examples

  • The lights are on, so someone must be at home.The lights are on, so it is almost certain that someone is at home.
  • She isn't answering her phone. She might be in a meeting.It is possible that she is in a meeting.
  • That can't be Sarah's car — she doesn't drive.It is impossible that it is Sarah's car.
  • He looks very pale. He must feel ill.The evidence strongly suggests he feels ill.
  • The keys aren't on the table. I might have left them in my coat.It is possible that I left them in my coat.
  • You can't have finished already — you only started ten minutes ago!It is impossible that you have finished already.

Typical mistakes

He must knows the answer.
He must know the answer.
After a modal verb we use the base verb without -s.
She mustn't be at work — her car isn't there.
She can't be at work — her car isn't there.
For a negative deduction we use can't, not mustn't; mustn't means 'it is not allowed'.
They must to be on holiday.
They must be on holiday.
We do not use to after a modal verb.

Check yourself

  1. You've been working all day. You ___ be exhausted.

  2. I'm not sure where Tom is. He ___ be in the garden.

  3. That ___ be John's bag — his name is on it.

  4. She ___ have written this email — she was on holiday that day.

  5. The window is broken. Someone ___ have climbed in.
