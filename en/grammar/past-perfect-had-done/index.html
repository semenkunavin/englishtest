Past Perfect (had done) — English Grammar Lesson B1

The past perfect is had + past participle (had done, had gone, had seen). We use it to talk about something that happened before another action or time in the past. It makes the order of past events clear.

  1. Form: had + past participle for all persons: I had finished, she had left, they had eaten. Short form: I'd finished.
  2. Negative: had not (hadn't) + past participle: He hadn't seen the film.
  3. Question: had + subject + past participle: Had you eaten before you left?
  4. Use it for the earlier of two past actions: When I arrived, the train had left. (train left first, then I arrived)
  5. Use it with time words: before, after, already, never, by the time, when: By the time we got there, the film had started.
  6. Do not use it for a single past action with no earlier action: say I saw him yesterday, not I had seen him yesterday.

Examples

  • When I got to the station, the train had already left.When I got to the station, the train had already left.
  • She had never seen the sea before that trip.She had never seen the sea before that trip.
  • By the time we arrived, the film had started.By the time we arrived, the film had started.
  • He told me he had lost his keys.He told me he had lost his keys.
  • They had finished dinner before I called them.They had finished dinner before I called them.
  • Had you met her before the party?Had you met her before the party?

Typical mistakes

When I arrived, the train already left.
When I arrived, the train had already left.
The train left before I arrived, so we need had + past participle.
I had went to London last year.
I had gone to London last year.
After had use the past participle gone, not the past simple went.
I had seen him yesterday at the park.
I saw him yesterday at the park.
A single finished past action with no earlier action takes the past simple, not the past perfect.
