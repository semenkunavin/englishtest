Present Perfect vs Past Simple — A2 English Guide

The present perfect and the past simple are both used to talk about the past, but they answer different questions. The past simple says when something happened; the present perfect connects the past to now and does not say exactly when.

  1. Use the past simple for a finished action at a known time: I saw him yesterday.
  2. Use the present perfect for a past action with no exact time, especially when the result matters now: I have lost my keys.
  3. Use the present perfect with ever, never, already, just and yet: Have you ever been to London?
  4. Use the past simple with time words like yesterday, last week, in 2019, two days ago: We moved here in 2019.
  5. Use the present perfect for a situation that started in the past and continues now, often with for and since: I have lived here for five years.
  6. Form: past simple = verb + -ed or irregular form (worked, went); present perfect = have/has + past participle (have worked, has gone).

Examples

  • I have just finished my homework.I have just finished my homework.
  • She has never eaten sushi.She has never eaten sushi.
  • We went to Paris last summer.We went to Paris last summer.
  • He has worked here since 2020.He has worked here since 2020.
  • Did you see the match yesterday?Did you see the match yesterday?
  • They have bought a new car.They have bought a new car.

Typical mistakes

I have seen him yesterday.
I saw him yesterday.
Yesterday is a finished time, so use the past simple.
She has went to the shops.
She has gone to the shops.
After have/has use the past participle gone, not the past simple went.
Did you ever be to Rome?
Have you ever been to Rome?
Use the present perfect with ever for life experience.
