Possessive 's: Anna's Phone and Other People's Things

The possessive 's shows that something belongs to a person. You add 's to a name or a noun for a person: Anna's phone means the phone of Anna. We use it all the time to talk about people's things, family and friends.

  1. Add 's to a singular person: Anna's phone, my brother's car.
  2. For regular plural nouns ending in -s, add only an apostrophe: my parents' house, the students' books.
  3. For irregular plurals, add 's: the children's room, the men's bags.
  4. The 's comes after the owner and before the thing: Tom's dog = the dog of Tom.
  5. Use 's mainly for people and animals, not for things: say the door of the car, not the car's door.
  6. In questions, use Whose: Whose bag is this? It's Maria's.

Examples

  • Anna's phone is on the table.The phone that belongs to Anna is on the table.
  • My sister's name is Emma.The name of my sister is Emma.
  • This is my parents' house.This house belongs to my parents.
  • The children's toys are in the box.The toys that belong to the children are in the box.
  • Is this your friend's bike?Does this bike belong to your friend?
  • Whose jacket is this? It's Tom's.Who owns this jacket? It belongs to Tom.

Typical mistakes

Anna phone is new.
Anna's phone is new.
You need 's after the owner's name.
This is my parents's car.
This is my parents' car.
With a plural noun ending in -s, add only the apostrophe.
The phone's Anna is new.
Anna's phone is new.
The owner comes first with 's, then the thing.

Check yourself

  2. My ___ car is red. (parents)

  3. The ___ room is big. (children)

  4. ___ book is this? (question word)

  5. Look at ___ new phone! (David)
