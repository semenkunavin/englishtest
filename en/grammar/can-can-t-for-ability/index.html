Can and Can't for Ability: A1 English Grammar

We use can and can't to talk about ability: things you are able to do. We also use them to say if something is possible or impossible. The form is the same for I, you, he, she, it, we and they.

  1. Use can + base verb for ability: I can swim, she can cook.
  2. The form of can never changes: he can, not he cans.
  3. Negative: can't (cannot). Say I can't drive.
  4. Can goes before the subject in questions: Can you swim? Short answer: Yes, I can / No, I can't.
  5. After can, use the verb without to: I can to swim is wrong; I can swim is correct.
  6. To talk about the past or future, use could or will be able to: I could swim at five; I will be able to come tomorrow.

Examples

  • I can swim very well.I can swim very well.
  • She can speak three languages.She can speak three languages.
  • We can't come to the party tonight.We can't come to the party tonight.
  • Can you play the guitar?Can you play the guitar?
  • My brother can't cook, but he can bake bread.My brother can't cook, but he can bake bread.
  • They can help you with your homework.They can help you with your homework.

Typical mistakes

She cans sing.
She can sing.
The form of can never changes, even with he, she or it.
I can to drive.
I can drive.
After can, use the verb without to.
Do you can swim?
Can you swim?
Can is the question word: put can before the subject and do not use do.
