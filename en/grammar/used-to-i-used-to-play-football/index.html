Used to — Talking About Past Habits in English (A2)

We use used to to talk about things that happened regularly in the past but do not happen now. It is also for past situations that are not true today. The form is the same for all persons: I used to, he used to, they used to.

  1. Use used to + base verb for past habits: I used to play football every Saturday.
  2. Use used to for past situations that are not true now: She used to live in London.
  3. Negative: didn't use to (no -d): I didn't use to like coffee.
  4. Questions: Did you use to ...? (no -d): Did you use to play tennis?
  5. The form never changes: he used to, she used to, they used to.
  6. There is no present form of used to. For the present, use the present simple: I play football every Saturday.

Examples

  • I used to play football in the park when I was a child.I used to play football in the park when I was a child.
  • She used to live in a small village near the sea.She used to live in a small village near the sea.
  • We didn't use to have a car, so we walked everywhere.We didn't use to have a car, so we walked everywhere.
  • Did you use to watch cartoons on Saturday mornings?Did you use to watch cartoons on Saturday mornings?
  • My brother used to be very shy when he was at school.My brother used to be very shy when he was at school.
  • They used to visit their grandmother every summer.They used to visit their grandmother every summer.

Typical mistakes

I use to play football.
I used to play football.
In positive sentences, used to always has the -d.
Did you used to play tennis?
Did you use to play tennis?
After did, use use to without the -d.
I am used to play football every Saturday.
I used to play football every Saturday.
Be used to means 'be familiar with something' and is followed by an -ing form. For past habits, use used to + base verb.
