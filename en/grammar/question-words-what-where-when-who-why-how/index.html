Question Words in English: what, where, when, who, why, how

Question words go at the start of a question and tell us what kind of information the answer gives. We use what, where, when, who, why and how in everyday questions about things, places, times, people, reasons and ways.

  1. Put the question word first, then the verb: Where is the bus? / What is your name?
  2. What asks about a thing or information: What is this?
  3. Where asks about a place: Where do you live?
  4. When asks about a time or day: When is the class?
  5. Who asks about a person: Who is she?
  6. Why asks about a reason (often answered with because) and How asks about the way or condition: Why are you sad? / How are you?

Examples

  • What is your favourite food?What is your favourite food?
  • Where is the train station?Where is the train station?
  • When does the film start?When does the film start?
  • Who is that man in the blue coat?Who is that man in the blue coat?
  • Why are you late today?Why are you late today?
  • How do you go to work?How do you go to work?

Typical mistakes

What you are doing?
What are you doing?
In questions the verb be comes before the subject: are you, not you are.
Where you live?
Where do you live?
With most verbs in the present simple we need do after the question word: Where do you live?
Who is she a teacher?
Is she a teacher?
Who is for people, not things or jobs here. Use who only when you ask about a person, e.g. Who is that woman?

Check yourself

  1. ___ is your name?

  2. ___ do you live?

  3. ___ is your birthday?

  4. ___ is your best friend?

  5. ___ are you crying?
