Phrasal Verbs: Separable and Inseparable (B1)

A phrasal verb is a verb plus a particle such as up, off or on. Some phrasal verbs are separable: you can put the object between the verb and the particle. Others are inseparable: the verb and particle must stay together. Knowing the difference helps you sound natural and avoid common mistakes.

  1. With separable verbs, the object can go after the particle or between the verb and the particle: turn off the TV / turn the TV off.
  2. With inseparable verbs, the object must come after the particle: look after the baby, never look the baby after.
  3. When the object is a pronoun (it, them, him, her), separable verbs must be split: turn it off, not turn off it.
  4. Three-word phrasal verbs are always inseparable: get on with, look forward to, put up with.
  5. Some verbs have two meanings: one separable and one inseparable. For example, run into (meet) is inseparable, but run + into as a movement verb is also inseparable. Check the meaning in a dictionary.
  6. If you are not sure, keep the verb and particle together: pick up the phone is always correct with separable verbs.

Examples

  • Please turn off the lights before you leave.Please turn the lights off before you leave. (separable)
  • I can't find my keys. Can you help me look for them?Look for is inseparable, so the object comes after for.
  • She looked after her little brother all weekend.Look after is inseparable: we never say look her brother after.
  • Can you pick it up for me?With a pronoun object, separable verbs must be split: pick it up.
  • I don't know how she puts up with his behaviour.Put up with is a three-word phrasal verb, so it is inseparable.
  • He turned the music down because the neighbours complained.Turn down is separable: turn down the music or turn the music down.

Typical mistakes

Can you turn off it?
Can you turn it off?
With a pronoun object, separable verbs must be split.
She looked her brother after.
She looked after her brother.
Look after is inseparable, so the object cannot go in the middle.
I can't put up his rudeness with.
I can't put up with his rudeness.
Three-word phrasal verbs are inseparable; the particle stays with the verb.

  2. She ___ her grandmother ___ every Sunday.

  3. These shoes are too small. Can I ___ them ___?

  4. He ___ his colleagues ___ very well.
