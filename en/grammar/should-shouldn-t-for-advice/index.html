Should and Shouldn't for Advice in English (A2)

We use should and shouldn't to give advice or say what is a good or bad idea. We use them when a friend has a problem and we want to help. The form is simple: should + verb, with no 'to'.

  1. Use should + base verb to give advice: You should rest.
  2. Use shouldn't (= should not) + base verb to say something is a bad idea: You shouldn't smoke.
  3. The form is the same for all people: I should, he should, they should.
  4. Do not use to after should: You should go, not <s>You should to go</s>.
  5. For questions, put should before the subject: Should I call her?
  6. Short answers use just should or shouldn't: Yes, you should. / No, you shouldn't.

Examples

  • You should drink more water.You should drink more water.
  • She should see a doctor.She should see a doctor.
  • You shouldn't eat so much sugar.You shouldn't eat so much sugar.
  • We should leave early tomorrow.We should leave early tomorrow.
  • Should I tell him the truth?Should I tell him the truth?
  • They shouldn't drive so fast.They shouldn't drive so fast.

Typical mistakes

You should to take a break.
You should take a break.
We use the base verb after should, without 'to'.
He shoulds study more.
He should study more.
Should never takes -s; the form is the same for all people.
You don't should worry.
You shouldn't worry.
The negative is shouldn't (should not), not 'don't should'.

  3. He has a bad tooth. He ___ eat sweets.

  4. ___ I open the window? It's hot in here.

  5. You ___ tell lies. It's not good.
