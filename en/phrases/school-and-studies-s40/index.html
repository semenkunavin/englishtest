School and Studies in English: phrases and a sample dialogue

These phrases help you talk about school subjects, describe a favourite teacher and explain your study plans. Use them in class, with friends or in a short conversation about school.

Useful phrases

  • What's your favourite subject?What's your favourite subject?
  • My favourite subject is history.My favourite subject is history.
  • I'm good at maths, but I'm not very good at science.I'm good at maths, but I'm not very good at science.
  • I find English quite easy.I find English quite easy.
  • I find physics a bit difficult.I find physics a bit difficult.
  • We have five lessons on Monday.We have five lessons on Monday.
  • The lesson starts at nine o'clock.The lesson starts at nine o'clock.
  • Our teacher is very patient and helpful.Our teacher is very patient and helpful.
  • She explains things clearly.She explains things clearly.
  • He makes the lessons interesting.He makes the lessons interesting.
  • Who is your favourite teacher?Who is your favourite teacher?
  • My favourite teacher is Mr Brown.My favourite teacher is Mr Brown.
  • Why do you like her?Why do you like her?
  • I like her because she is kind and funny.I like her because she is kind and funny.
  • I'm planning to study harder this term.I'm planning to study harder this term.
  • I want to improve my English.I want to improve my English.
  • I'd like to go to university one day.I'd like to go to university one day.
  • I'm thinking about studying abroad.I'm thinking about studying abroad.
  • What are your plans for next year?What are your plans for next year?
  • I need to prepare for my exams.I need to prepare for my exams.

Sample dialogue

  • Partner: Hi, Sam. What's your favourite subject this year?Hi, Sam. What's your favourite subject this year?
  • You: Hi! My favourite subject is history. I find it really interesting.Hi! My favourite subject is history. I find it really interesting.
  • Partner: Good choice. And which subject is difficult for you?Good choice. And which subject is difficult for you?
  • You: Physics. I'm not very good at it, but I'm working on it.Physics. I'm not very good at it, but I'm working on it.
  • Partner: That's the right attitude. Who is your favourite teacher?That's the right attitude. Who is your favourite teacher?
  • You: Mr Brown. He explains things clearly and makes the lessons fun.Mr Brown. He explains things clearly and makes the lessons fun.
  • Partner: Nice. Do you have any study plans for next term?Nice. Do you have any study plans for next term?
  • You: Yes, I'm planning to study harder and improve my English.Yes, I'm planning to study harder and improve my English.
  • Partner: That sounds great. What about after school?That sounds great. What about after school?
  • You: I'd like to go to university one day, so I need to prepare for my exams.I'd like to go to university one day, so I need to prepare for my exams.
  • Partner: Good luck! Come and ask me if you need help.Good luck! Come and ask me if you need help.
  • You: Thank you. I will!Thank you. I will!

Typical mistakes

I am good in English.
I am good at English.
We use 'good at' before a subject or skill.
My favourite subject is the history.
My favourite subject is history.
Don't use 'the' before school subjects like history or maths.
I want study abroad.
I want to study abroad.
After 'want' we use 'to' plus the verb.

Tip

Keep your answers short and add one reason: 'I like history because it's interesting.' This sounds natural and helps you speak longer.
