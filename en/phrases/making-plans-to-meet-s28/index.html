Making plans to meet in English: phrases and a sample dialogue

These phrases help you make plans with friends in everyday English: choosing a day, a time and a place, and changing the plan if something comes up. They are short, natural and easy to use in texts or in person.

Sample dialogue

  • Partner: Hey! Are you free on Saturday?Hey! Are you free on Saturday?
  • You: Yes, I am. What did you have in mind?Yes, I am. What did you have in mind?
  • Partner: Do you want to meet up for coffee?Do you want to meet up for coffee?
  • You: Sounds good! What time works for you?Sounds good! What time works for you?
  • Partner: How about 3 p.m. at the station café?How about 3 p.m. at the station café?
  • You: That works for me. See you there!That works for me. See you there!
  • Partner: Sorry, something's come up. Can we meet a bit later?Sorry, something's come up. Can we meet a bit later?
  • You: No problem. How about 5 instead?No problem. How about 5 instead?
  • Partner: Perfect. Same place?Perfect. Same place?
  • You: Yes, same place. Text me when you're there.Yes, same place. Text me when you're there.

Typical mistakes

Are you free in Saturday?
Are you free on Saturday?
Use 'on' with days of the week.
I will meet you in the station.
I will meet you at the station.
Use 'at' for a meeting point or place.
We meet at 6 o'clock?
Shall we meet at 6 o'clock? / Are we meeting at 6?
A question needs a question form or a suggestion word like 'Shall we'.

Tip

Repeat the day, time and place back to your friend at the end ('Saturday at 5, at the station café') so you both know the plan.
