IELTS Speaking Part 1: Your Hometown in English: Phrases and Sample Dialogue

These phrases help you answer short questions about your home and hometown in IELTS Speaking Part 1. You can use them to give 2–3 sentence answers.

Useful phrases

  • Where are you from?Where are you from?
  • I'm from...I'm from...
  • I come from...I come from...
  • It's a small town near...It's a small town near...
  • It's a big city in the north/south/east/west of...It's a big city in the north/south/east/west of...
  • What do you like about your hometown?What do you like about your hometown?
  • I like the friendly people.I like the friendly people.
  • The best thing is the food.The best thing is the food.
  • It's famous for its beautiful parks.It's famous for its beautiful parks.
  • Is there anything you don't like?Is there anything you don't like?
  • The traffic is terrible.The traffic is terrible.
  • It's very noisy and crowded.It's very noisy and crowded.
  • How long have you lived there?How long have you lived there?
  • I've lived there all my life.I've lived there all my life.
  • I moved there when I was...I moved there when I was...
  • Would you like to live there in the future?Would you like to live there in the future?
  • Yes, definitely. It's a great place to live.Yes, definitely. It's a great place to live.
  • Maybe, but I'd like to try living in another city first.Maybe, but I'd like to try living in another city first.
  • What is your hometown like?What is your hometown like?
  • It's a quiet place with a lot of history.It's a quiet place with a lot of history.

Sample dialogue

  • Partner: Where are you from?Where are you from?
  • You: I'm from a small town called Greenfield. It's in the north of the country, near the mountains.I'm from a small town called Greenfield. It's in the north of the country, near the mountains.
  • Partner: What do you like about your hometown?What do you like about your hometown?
  • You: I like the friendly people and the clean air. The best thing is the local food—it's delicious.I like the friendly people and the clean air. The best thing is the local food—it's delicious.
  • Partner: Is there anything you don't like about it?Is there anything you don't like about it?
  • You: Yes, the public transport is not very good. There are only a few buses, so it's difficult to travel around.Yes, the public transport is not very good. There are only a few buses, so it's difficult to travel around.
  • Partner: How long have you lived there?How long have you lived there?
  • You: I've lived there all my life. I was born there and I grew up there.I've lived there all my life. I was born there and I grew up there.
  • Partner: Would you like to live there in the future?Would you like to live there in the future?
  • You: Maybe, but I'd like to try living in a bigger city first. I think it would be more exciting.Maybe, but I'd like to try living in a bigger city first. I think it would be more exciting.

Typical mistakes

I am from a small town which it is near the mountains.
I am from a small town which is near the mountains.
Don't use 'it' after 'which' when 'which' is the subject.
I like my hometown because the people are friendly and the food is delicious and the weather is nice.
I like my hometown because the people are friendly and the food is delicious. The weather is also nice.
Break long sentences into shorter ones for clarity.
I have lived here since I was born.
I have lived here all my life.
Use 'all my life' for a simple answer. 'Since I was born' is also correct but less common in speaking.

Tip

Give 2–3 sentence answers: one sentence for the direct answer, and one or two more for extra details or examples. This shows you can speak at length.
