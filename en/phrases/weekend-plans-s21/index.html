Weekend plans in English: phrases and a sample dialogue

These phrases help you talk with friends about what to do at the weekend. You can use them to make suggestions, ask about plans, and agree on something together.

Useful phrases

  • What are you up to this weekend?What are your plans for this weekend?
  • Do you have any plans for Saturday?Do you have anything planned for Saturday?
  • I'm not sure yet.I don't know my plans yet.
  • I've got nothing planned.I have no plans.
  • Do you fancy going for a coffee?Would you like to go for a coffee?
  • How about going to the cinema?What do you think about going to the cinema?
  • We could meet up on Sunday.It's possible for us to meet on Sunday.
  • Shall we meet at the park?Do you want us to meet at the park?
  • That sounds great.I like that idea.
  • That works for me.That time or plan is fine for me.
  • I'm free on Saturday afternoon.I have no plans on Saturday afternoon.
  • Sorry, I can't make it on Saturday.Sorry, I'm not able to come on Saturday.
  • Maybe another time?Perhaps we can do it on a different day?
  • What time works for you?What time is good for you?
  • Let's say three o'clock.We agree on three o'clock.
  • I'll text you when I'm on my way.I will send you a message when I am coming.
  • See you on Saturday.Goodbye until Saturday.
  • Count me in.I want to join you.
  • I'm up for that.I would like to do that.
  • Let's decide on Friday.We will choose the plan on Friday.

Sample dialogue

  • Partner: Hi! What are you up to this weekend?Hi! What are your plans for this weekend?
  • You: I've got nothing planned yet. How about you?I have no plans yet. What about you?
  • Partner: I'm free on Saturday afternoon. Do you fancy going for a coffee?I have no plans on Saturday afternoon. Would you like to go for a coffee?
  • You: That sounds great. What time works for you?I like that idea. What time is good for you?
  • Partner: Let's say three o'clock at the café near the station.We agree on three o'clock at the café near the station.
  • You: That works for me. I'll text you when I'm on my way.That time is fine for me. I will send you a message when I am coming.
  • Partner: Perfect. Do you want to bring anyone else?Perfect. Do you want to invite anyone else?
  • You: Good idea. I'll ask Sam. Count him in!Good idea. I will ask Sam. He wants to join us!
  • Partner: Nice. See you on Saturday.Nice. Goodbye until Saturday.
  • You: See you then. Bye!Goodbye until then. Bye!

Typical mistakes

I am agree.
I agree. / That works for me.
In English, 'agree' is a verb, so you don't use 'am' before it.
How about go to the cinema?
How about going to the cinema?
After 'how about', use the -ing form of the verb.
I free on Saturday.
I'm free on Saturday.
You need the verb 'be' (am/is/are) before an adjective like 'free'.

Tip

When you make a suggestion, give a time and a place too — for example, 'How about a coffee at three at the café near the station?' It makes it much easier for your friend to say yes.
