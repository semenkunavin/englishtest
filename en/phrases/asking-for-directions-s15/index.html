Asking for directions in English: phrases and a sample dialogue

These phrases help you ask a stranger for directions and make sure you really understood the answer. They are useful in any city when you need to find a place like the train station.

Useful phrases

  • Excuse me, could you help me?Excuse me, could you help me?
  • Sorry to bother you.Sorry to bother you.
  • How do I get to the train station?How do I get to the train station?
  • Is the train station far from here?Is the train station far from here?
  • Can I walk there, or should I take a bus?Can I walk there, or should I take a bus?
  • Which way is it?Which way is it?
  • Go straight ahead.Go straight ahead.
  • Turn left at the traffic lights.Turn left at the traffic lights.
  • Take the second street on the right.Take the second street on the right.
  • It's next to the big supermarket.It's next to the big supermarket.
  • It's opposite the park.It's opposite the park.
  • It's about a ten-minute walk.It's about a ten-minute walk.
  • You can't miss it.You can't miss it.
  • Follow the signs.Follow the signs.
  • Sorry, could you say that again more slowly?Sorry, could you say that again more slowly?
  • So, first I go straight, then left?So, first I go straight, then left?
  • Do you mean the street with the bank?Do you mean the street with the bank?
  • Is it on this side of the road or the other side?Is it on this side of the road or the other side?
  • Thank you so much, that's very helpful.Thank you so much, that's very helpful.
  • Thanks a lot for your help.Thanks a lot for your help.

Sample dialogue

  • You: Excuse me, sorry to bother you. Could you help me?Excuse me, sorry to bother you. Could you help me?
  • Partner: Of course. What do you need?Of course. What do you need?
  • You: How do I get to the train station?How do I get to the train station?
  • Partner: Go straight ahead, then turn left at the traffic lights.Go straight ahead, then turn left at the traffic lights.
  • You: Sorry, could you say that again more slowly?Sorry, could you say that again more slowly?
  • Partner: Sure. Go straight ahead. Turn left at the lights. The station is opposite the park. About ten minutes on foot.Sure. Go straight ahead. Turn left at the lights. The station is opposite the park. About ten minutes on foot.
  • You: So, first I go straight, then left at the traffic lights?So, first I go straight, then left at the traffic lights?
  • Partner: That's right. It's opposite the park. You can't miss it.That's right. It's opposite the park. You can't miss it.
  • You: Great. And is it on this side of the road?Great. And is it on this side of the road?
  • Partner: No, it's on the other side, next to the supermarket.No, it's on the other side, next to the supermarket.
  • You: Thank you so much, that's very helpful.Thank you so much, that's very helpful.

Typical mistakes

How to get to the train station?
How do I get to the train station?
A question needs a subject and a verb: 'do I get'. 'How to get...' is not a full question.
Where is the train station at?
Where is the train station?
Don't add 'at' at the end. Native speakers say 'Where is it?' without 'at'.
I understand, but repeat please.
Sorry, could you say that again, please?
'Repeat please' sounds too direct. 'Could you say that again?' is a polite, natural way to ask.

Tip

Repeat the directions back to the person in your own words, for example 'So, straight, then left at the lights?' This checks that you understood and sounds friendly.
