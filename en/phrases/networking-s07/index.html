Networking: What Do You Do? in English: phrases and a sample dialogue

These phrases help you talk about your job at networking events, parties, or when you meet new people. You can explain what you do in a simple way and ask friendly questions about the other person's work.

Useful phrases

  • So, what do you do?So, what do you do?
  • What do you do for a living?What do you do for a living?
  • What kind of work are you in?What kind of work are you in?
  • Where do you work?Where do you work?
  • I work in sales.I work in sales.
  • I work for a small company.I work for a small company.
  • I'm a nurse.I'm a nurse.
  • I'm in charge of the design team.I'm in charge of the design team.
  • I take care of customer orders.I take care of customer orders.
  • I mostly work with numbers.I mostly work with numbers.
  • I help people find apartments.I help people find apartments.
  • Basically, I answer emails and plan meetings.Basically, I answer emails and plan meetings.
  • It's an office job.It's an office job.
  • How about you?How about you?
  • What about you?What about you?
  • That sounds interesting.That sounds interesting.
  • How long have you worked there?How long have you worked there?
  • Do you like it?Do you like it?
  • I've been doing this for three years.I've been doing this for three years.
  • It was nice to meet you.It was nice to meet you.

Sample dialogue

  • Partner: Hi, I'm Anna. I don't think we've met.Hi, I'm Anna. I don't think we've met.
  • You: Hi Anna, I'm Sam. Nice to meet you.Hi Anna, I'm Sam. Nice to meet you.
  • Partner: So, what do you do, Sam?So, what do you do, Sam?
  • You: I work for a small company. I'm in customer service.I work for a small company. I'm in customer service.
  • Partner: Oh, nice. What does that mean day to day?Oh, nice. What does that mean day to day?
  • You: Basically, I answer emails and help people with their orders. How about you?Basically, I answer emails and help people with their orders. How about you?
  • Partner: I'm a designer. I work on websites for different clients.I'm a designer. I work on websites for different clients.
  • You: That sounds interesting. Do you like it?That sounds interesting. Do you like it?
  • Partner: Yes, I really do. How long have you worked at your company?Yes, I really do. How long have you worked at your company?
  • You: For about two years now.For about two years now.
  • Partner: Nice. Well, it was great to meet you, Sam.Nice. Well, it was great to meet you, Sam.
  • You: You too. Enjoy the event!You too. Enjoy the event!

Typical mistakes

What is your work?
What do you do?
This is the natural question in English; 'What is your work?' sounds unnatural.
I am working in a bank.
I work in a bank.
Use the present simple for your regular job; the continuous sounds temporary.
I am a work in sales.
I work in sales.
Don't use 'a' with 'work'; say 'I work in…' or 'I'm a sales manager.'

Tip

Prepare one short sentence about your job before the event, for example: 'I work in IT and I help fix computer problems.' Simple is fine — people just want a starting point for conversation.
